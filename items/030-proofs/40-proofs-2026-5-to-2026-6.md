# 030-40. The proofs from release 2026.5 to 2026.6

Created 2026-11-24 by Frans Lundberg. Status: done (a record).

A review, in the order it happened, of BergDB's proof work between
release 2026.5 (tagged on the morning of 13 November 2026) and release
2026.6 (planned for 24 November): what was proved and when, how the work
went, and the change of approach on 17 November that made it converge.
Written at Frans's request on the morning of the release, from the day
reports (006-24, 006-25, 006-27, 006-29 to 006-33), the status report of
14 November (006-26), 030's map, the items cited and the history of
`lean/` on `main`.

030-32 tells 16 to 20 November as a diary, and 030-26, section 7, gives
the short history of the public claim; this item covers the whole period,
the old line before the change included. 026-28 tells the same story as
an overview, for the way of working.

Frans's words of 17 and 18 November are quoted with his approval
(2026-11-24), as the day reports 006-30 and 006-31 recorded them.

The words are 006-26's: **proved**, a Lean theorem with no premise left
open; **reduced**, derived from named premises that are not proved;
**tested**, checked on runs or traces, with no theorem.


# 1. In short

- **13 to 16 November, the old line.** Java's old writer was proved step
  by step against the tree model's invariants. The work grew fast, to
  279,000 lines of Lean, but its top theorem for gets and scans stayed
  reduced: 17 premises, then 10, then 4. Three times a premise turned out
  false as stated, which left a top theorem vacuous. The writer was never
  proved.
- **17 November, the turning point.** Frans considered pausing the
  proofs. The contract approach replaced the old line: what users rely on
  is proved from a contract on the state after each transaction, and the
  contract is checked on Java after every transaction. Reads were proved
  from it in about an hour and a half.
- **18 and 19 November, a new writer.** The old writer resisted a proof
  against the contract: its puts were proved, its deletes were not. PE,
  "plan, then emit", a writer designed to be proved, was tried in the
  model, proved in one night and made Java's writer.
- **19 November, the aim reached.** Reclaim's cuts were proved safe. The
  core algorithms are proved, given tested assumptions (030-26).
- **20 to 23 November, keep it true.** The tested links were made
  stronger, the dirty-node bound was proved for most transactions, and
  the guarantees of 2026.6 became a closed list, G1 to G14, nine proved
  and five tested, reviewed independently (030-38, 030-39).

# 3. The old line: the old writer, step by step (13 to 16 November)

Where 2026.5 left it: the abstract model (the stream, the dirty set D,
startup, reclaim's cut, lazy reading, eviction with handles) had
recovery, restarts, lazy reading, eviction and safe cuts proved for any
index that meets its assumptions on each transaction. The key tree model
(024-12, 024-22, since 11 November) was a definition of Java's writer
that runs on Java's traces and gives Java's nodes exactly. The goal was
TP7, TP10 and TP11: after any run, the tree keeps its invariants, and
every get and scan at every time returns what a plain map returns.

## 3.1 Friday 13 November

- In the morning, TP11's scan fuel was taken from the parameters (option
  C), so that the reads' statements hold for every key length, and TP11
  was reduced to TP7 at the latest time. Codex runs and Claude provers
  worked on TP7, TP10, TP11, TP32, TP33 and T4 at once.
- T4 was proved (007; the records at the ends of the startup section),
  and at 14:02 `bounded_startup`, a cold start's reads bounded after any
  run that meets its explicit premises. At 16:14 Frans deferred bounded
  startup's premises for the tree: correctness first.
- The night, until 03:45: `lean/` grew by 142,295 lines in 683 commits to
  `main`. At 01:36 the top theorem, `invGetScanOK_merges_flat`: TP7, TP10
  and TP11 for Java's parameters at every node size from 8 up, reduced to
  17 named premises, 10 by 03:42, nine of them about deletes, each tested
  on runs with no failure. Every put case was proved, and LE-b proved
  never to run with the merges on, given the tree's live counts.
- The cost: the week's usage went from 0 to 72 % in that day and night.
  The proofs' cost against their value was discussed; on 15 November
  Frans decided that there was no spare-tokens policy and that the aim
  was still to prove everything (006-25, section 7).

## 3.2 Saturday 14 November

- The cleanup (025-02), one name per route: 296,684 to 209,065 lines,
  nothing proved changed.
- 006-26, the status report: gets and scans reduced to 10 premises; the
  abstract model's results reduced, not proved, for the tree; reclaim's
  keeping of the data reduced to "each cut is safe"; Java against the
  model tested only. One to three more nights were estimated.
- The night: three premises, `hCoU`, `hU` and `hHL`, were proved false
  as stated, in Lean. Each spoke of any writer state that meets a local
  invariant, and an unreachable state refuted it, so the top theorem held
  vacuously. Since then, statements speak of reached states (030, "The
  rules"). `hMF`, `hHL` and `hCoU` were removed with no statement change;
  nine premises were left, still vacuous through `hU`.

## 3.3 Sunday 15 November

- Unattended. `hU`, `hchk`, `d4`, `hMSF` and `hCoF` were removed by
  proofs and changes of their consumers (025-04, 025-05), with no
  statement changed; `htb` became `htbL`.
- The result, `invGetScanOK_merges_tbL`: from `hp` and `hth`, which
  Java's parameters meet, and two open premises, `l4` (a delete keeps the
  live counts) and `htbL` (a node that a delete takes back is below the
  fuel). None was known to be false. `lean/` went from 222,700 to 236,184
  lines; `l4` was estimated at 3,000 lines or more.

## 3.4 Monday 16 November

- 006-28, a study of simpler algorithms for simpler proofs; 2026.6's
  preliminary theme became "simpler algorithms".
- MT, maintenance transactions, passed every check of its trial in the
  model (026-17). Frans's idea of a main transaction made of
  subtransactions (026-18) had its proof trial in the night (026-19):
  reduced, not proved; a gain for the proofs neither supported nor
  refuted.
- 030 was made the home of all proof work (14:32).
- The night: `htbL` was proved (025-05, step 8), which left
  `invGetScanOK_merges_noTb`, from `hp`, `hth` and `l4`, as the best
  theorem with no premise known false. `l4` was cut into pieces by
  030-01's route B; the night's top theorem for it had four premises, one
  of which, `R3Calls`, was believed false at unreached states, so that
  theorem was believed vacuous. The bridges' plan (030-02) found
  two planned statements, TP14 and TP19, false as stated.
- On Tuesday morning the old line stood at 747 files and 279,136 lines.
  The writer was not proved; `l4`'s pieces, R3 and R4 were open.


# 4. The turning point: the contract (Tuesday 17 November)

- **09:13.** Frans considered pausing the proof work: "I am considering
  pausing the proof work. This is too much work from my side and too
  much token cost. Cost is too high for uncertain progress. I am about to
  make a public release of BergDB. [...] I want to be able to restart it
  in 12 months or so."
- **10:56 to 11:14.** Instead he chose the contract approach and a fresh
  start (030-04). On the contract, at 10:56: "It is 80-20. 80 percent of
  the value, 20 percent of the work. It is very practical. Being able to
  test each transaction in isolation is very strong. [...] I like this!"
  On a restart from scratch, at 11:00: "I think we might be stuck in too
  much dependence on history".
  - The contract: one statement of what holds when a transaction ends:
    the invariants, the old contents with the transaction's net change,
    and only the nodes in D changed.
  - Proved from the contract alone: every get and scan at every time,
    for any history of transactions that meet it; then recovery,
    restarts, eviction and cuts.
  - That the writer meets the contract: tested after every transaction,
    not proved.
  - Written from scratch, in a Lake package of its own, `lean/contract/`,
    with Mathlib. The old line was frozen at the tag
    `proofs-2026-11-17`. The Lean stays in the repository, labelled work
    in progress, and is not presented as a feature to users.
- **11:31 to 12:56, the spike** (030-05): `get_right`, `scan_right` and
  `inv_right` proved from the contract alone, in 4,890 lines, on the
  standard axioms; the contract held on 180 traces (21,000 transactions);
  the fill rule is not needed for reads. About an hour and a half and 4 %
  of a week.
- **The afternoon and evening**, each piece reviewed (030-06, 030-11 and
  three reviews by Codex):
  - 030-08: the top node and its skip pointers in the contract; Java's
    top search proved right, and Java's get and scan right at every time.
  - 030-09: Java checks the contract itself at each commit in its tests
    (about 4 million transactions, no failure); a trace that passes the
    checkers is proved to be a history that meets the contract.
  - 030-07: the bridge to the abstract model. Recovery, restarts, lazy
    reading, eviction with handles and reclaim's cut proved for the key
    tree from the contract, by the abstract model's existing proofs.
  - 030-14 and 030-17: reads after a crash, restarts, a lazy startup,
    eviction and cuts, end to end, with Java's readers too.
  - 030-12 and 030-13: the contract held on the large sets (170,000
    transactions); its checks run in `level2.sh` and `night.sh`.
- **18:00.** Frans celebrated with champagne: a practical Lean model,
  relying on a contract tested after every Java transaction. The work
  could have stopped there.
- **The evening: the old writer against the contract** (030-10, 030-16):
  every put proved, deletes only in part. The old line's hard cases (`l4`,
  R3, R4) did not come back, since the contract has no fill rule; what
  stayed hard was the delete's in-between states and its take-back.
- At 23:29: 195 theorems, 135 statement files pinned.


# 5. A writer made to be proved: PE (18 and 19 November)

- **08:42.** The old writer's proof was tagged
  `writer-proof-2026-11-18` and taken off `main`; the old line was
  deleted (030-18): `lean/` from 337,483 to 84,556 lines.
- **Why the old writer was hard** (026-20): it applied a transaction one
  operation at a time to the real tree. A node made by one operation
  could be split, merged or emptied by the next, and before the record
  was written the writer removed the nodes it had made and no longer
  needed: the take-back. Between operations the tree was half finished,
  and 13 to 14 sets of books described those states for the proof.
- **The morning's alternatives:**
  - 026-21, step times: each operation at its own internal time, no
    take-back; 1.12 times the bytes, 1.41 on sorted loads. Not taken;
    Frans, 11:23: "The first thought while reading it is that it is not
    worth it. The performance degradation from today limited but
    significant. A potentially smaller proof; that is a benefit. But the
    real gain would be to actually get a complete proof."
  - 026-22, MT against the contract: its in-between states fail the
    contract only by empty leaves.
  - 026-23, a second opinion from an agent that had not read the earlier
    studies: four proposals, first PE, "plan, then emit" (006-28's NW,
    never tried).
- **11:51.** The stable stream format was set aside for 2026.6; the
  release's focus became the algorithms, simplifying and proving them.
- **12:48 to 14:33, PE's trial** (026-24). PE plans the whole
  transaction on lists (each touched node's final list) and then writes
  each planned node once; no node is made and taken back. No fault on
  288 traces (221,142 transactions); 0.96 to 0.99 of the old writer's
  bytes; the model's writer about 280 code lines against 829; a full
  proof estimated at 6,500 to 9,500 lines. Go.
- **13:12**, while the trial ran, the aim was set (030, "The aim"): the
  core algorithms are proved, given tested assumptions; left for it, the
  writer meets the contract, and reclaim chooses only safe cuts.
- **21:42.** Frans: "Go! Good luck with the proof. If you successfully
  prove it, feel free to do the Java implementation too."
- **21:43 to 05:23, PE proved** (030-21):
  - 02:08: every history PE writes from the empty tree meets the contract
    with the tree and the top (`pe_meetsTop_start`), in 15,003 lines.
  - 04:42: the full contract (`pe_full`): recovery, restarts, lazy
    reading, eviction and safe cuts hold for PE.
  - 05:23: PE accepts every transaction whose keys the tree can hold,
    within named bounds (`pe_accepts`, `pe_refuses`).
  - About 23,000 lines in `lean/contract/`, 142 theorems, no existing
    statement changed.
- **02:10 to 05:04, Java's writer as PE** (026-25), node for node the
  proved one, record format 8. The take-back and its books, the split
  variants, the upward unlink and the merges at the delete were removed;
  `Dag.java` went from 1,892 code lines to 782, and `PeWriter.java` is
  new. The checkers' copy of PE showed no
  difference from the proved writer.
- **19 November, 09:32.** After timing runs on quiet machines, Frans
  decided to merge it; on `main` at 09:48 (9bcbeea8). Since then the
  writer that ships is the proved one.
- **The cost in speed** (028-19, open): `timing.sh` of 21 November
  against 2026.5 gave writes with reclaim 10 to 27 % faster, writes
  without reclaim 6 to 10 % slower, reads 1 to 5 % slower, CORE 1.060.
- **Found on the way**, before any release: Java's `dirtyNodeBound` did
  not bound PE in three put-only cases (028-21, fixed), and reclaim's
  charge missed the index nodes PE makes after the row (028-26, from
  Codex's review of Java's PE, fixed).


# 6. Reclaim, and the aim reached (19 November)

- 030-22: reclaim's contract, four conditions at each cut, RC1 to RC4,
  that a trace can test.
- 030-23 (13:12): T-SCHEME stated and half proved. The invariant on
  superseded versions for the other half came from an independent search
  by Codex.
- 030-24 (15:13): reads are right after reclaim's cuts, for every history
  PE writes (`PE.pe_reclaim_reads`).
- 030-25 (15:51): reclaim's iteration proved from a model of its steps
  (`PE.pe_reclaim_reads_steps`). With 030-21 and 030-24, **the aim was
  reached**.
- The same day: independent reviews of the statements of 030-21, 030-24
  and 030-25 found no statement defect, and the prose that said more was
  corrected; every reclaim step on the traces was checked against the
  model (030-27); the checkers' copy of PE was compared with the proved
  writer at every transaction (030-28).
- Frans chose the public claim, "BergDB's core algorithms are proved",
  with the model and its limits stated beside it (030-26), and 2026.6's
  theme, proven algorithms.


# 7. Keep it true, strengthen the links (20 to 23 November)

From 20 November the goals were to keep 030-26 true, to strengthen the
tested links where a fault would hurt users most, and to design 2026.8's
stream format model first (030, "The aim").

- 030-29 (20 November): Java's `dirtyNodeBound` proved to bound PE's
  writes for transactions of puts, and PE proved to accept every
  transaction Java admits, at Java's height bound (`pe_accepts_java`).
- 030-30 and 030-31: new trace seeds every night, Java's commit check in
  `week.sh`, and the user apps' workloads and reclaim across 943 restarts
  and 167 crashes checked against the model.
- 030-33, an independent review of the statements (Codex): the model of
  reclaim's steps did not require a step to write back the value it read,
  so a wrong value written by maintenance would have been accepted. Java
  copies the value. Closed in the checker (030-34) and in the proof
  (030-35, `pe_reclaim_user_reads_steps`): reads after cuts are proved
  against the users' operations alone.
- 030-36 (20 November) and 030-37 (21 November): the dirty-node bound
  for deletes without joins, then through every leaf join and one level
  of inner joins. The joins of inner nodes at height two and above stay
  tested; goal 2 was declared done on 23 November.
- 030-38 (23 November): the guarantees of 2026.6 as a closed list, G1 to
  G14. Its independent review (030-39, Codex) found nine places that said
  more than is proved or checked; each was corrected, or accepted with
  its reason. Two gaps are proposed for acceptance in 2026.6: the
  reclaim theorem's run restarts at every cut, and the admission count
  for value transformations is argued, not proved.
- At the release: 254 theorems on Lean's three standard axioms, about
  68,000 lines in `lean/contract/`; commit A gated (026-27).


# 8. What release 2026.6 has

The list is 030-38's; in short:

- **Proved**, for a model of BergDB and given the tested assumptions of
  030-26, section 5: a transaction is applied whole (G1); reads at the
  latest time and at every earlier one (G2); after a crash, the database
  as after the last whole record (G3); restarts and eviction change
  nothing (G4, G5); reads right after reclaim's cuts, and reclaim keeps
  the users' values (G6, G7); every transaction Java admits is one the
  proved writer accepts (G8); its index nodes fit the count Java checks,
  for puts and for joins up to one level above the leaves (G9).
- **Tested on purpose:** the higher inner joins (G10), a refused
  transaction changes nothing (G11), synced transactions survive a crash
  with at most a torn tail (G12), the bytes of values (G13), readers
  beside the writer (G14).
- **Outside:** the Pub layer, threads beyond G14, costs, time, memory and
  pauses, damaged bytes, and release 2026.5 and earlier, whose writer is
  another.


# 9. How the work went

- **Before 17 November, fast but not converging.** Each night removed
  premises and added tens of thousands of lines. The proofs followed
  every in-between state of the writer, so each new case brought new
  books and new premises, and three times a premise was found false as
  stated. One day and night took 72 % of a week's usage. After four days
  the writer was not proved and the distance left was unknown.
- **The contract changed the shape of the problem.** It separates what
  users rely on, a few small proofs that do not change when the writer
  does, from how the writer works, which is checked after every
  transaction, in Java's own tests and on recorded traces. The gap
  between model and code became a test that runs all the time, not a
  matter of trust.
- **The algorithm was changed to fit the proof**, not the proof to the
  algorithm. PE has no in-between states, so its proof follows one
  recursion over the tree; it was proved in one night. The price was a
  few per cent of speed in some workloads, and a gain in others.
- **Small trials before large efforts.** The contract's spike took an
  hour and a half and PE's trial two hours, each with criteria that
  could refute it, before a night of proof was spent.
- **Fresh and independent views.** PE came from an agent that had not
  read the earlier studies. The independent reviews of statements, not
  of proofs, found where the prose said more than the theorems, and once
  (030-33) a real gap in the model.
- **Size.** The old line reached 279,000 lines without proving the
  writer. The new line proves the writer and reclaim in about 68,000
  lines, and `lean/` as a whole is less than half its peak.
