Review of the start and recovery path, 2026-11-28
==================================================

Created 2026-11-28 by Frans Lundberg. Status: done; the findings are for
028 and 033 to register.

An independent review of BergDB's start and recovery on `main` at
`ee8a8165` (2026-11-28), after 028-67's C1 and C3 with T1's fix, 028-66's
option C with the rule of the files before a gap, 028-76's first valid
block, 028-60's zero tail and 028-62's file sizes. The question: which
sequences of kills, power losses, starts, stops, cuts and a recovery's
own writes make a start refuse a sound database, take another file's
bytes, lose an accepted record, or write before it refuses. The rules it
was read against are 027-03, sections 6 and 7, 027-11 and 014-01.

Method: the code read (`RecoverableStream`, `FileStream`,
`StoredTrans.RECORD_CHECK`, `NodeStream`, `Db.startInner`,
`Reclaim.initFromDb` and the cut), then probes, in
`2026-11-28-start-probes/` beside this file; section 3 has the runs.
Severities are 033's (S1 data lost or wrong, S2 the database will not
start, S3 a call refuses wrongly, S4 minor). Nothing in `java/` or
`lean/` was changed.


1. Findings
===========

Ranked by severity. F1 and F2 need the bytes of another file in the last
stream file after a kill, the environment of 035-02 and 028-67 (seen on
APFS RAM disks and disk images, not on F53's internal SSD); each says
what more it needs. In the environment of zeros and cut tails alone
there are F3 and F4 only: section 2.

F1. A copy of the database passes C3's checks
---------------------------------------------

Severity S1, in 035-02's environment and with a deleted copy of the
database's later files on the same volume, as a restore of a backup in
place leaves them. Shown by probes on constructed images
(`StaleCopyProbe`; `StaleFirstBlockProbe OUT sameid exact`).

Where: `StoredTrans.headerCheck`
(`java/src/bergdb/core/StoredTrans.java:110`) and `MetaBlock.isOwn`
(`java/src/bergdb/storage/MetaBlock.java:262`) tie a record and a block
to the database's ID and their position. A copy of the database's files
has the same ID and the same positions: its later records and blocks at
the same positions hold their checks in the database it was copied from.
`Database`'s Javadoc ("What a crash keeps") says that "a start takes no
meta block or record of another file or place"; that holds for another
database or place, not for another file. `DbTesterTail.testS1Refused`
notes that 031-07's probe had two databases of one ID, the test
setup's, and that "another ID is what separates two real databases"; a
copy of a database is a real one with the same ID.

The case: a backup is restored in place (the database's files deleted,
the backup's put there) and the database started and written. The
deleted files, A's, hold what was committed after the backup, at the
positions where the restored database, B, writes next, and A's stream
files after the backup's last have the names of the files that B makes
next. A kill that cuts the first write of such a new file can leave A's
bytes there, at the same offsets (035-02, section 7.1: from 2 MiB into
the write, so in a file of 5 MB or more; 2 of its 6 cases were at the
same offsets), which here are also the same positions. Three outcomes,
by where the bytes begin:

* **The refusal's P is before committed records.** A's block at a meta
  position among those bytes (any, not only the file's first) is valid,
  with B's ID and its own position: the start takes the last of them as
  the last valid block and walks from the record that it names, a
  position in A. If B has no record's start there, the walk
  refuses the bytes at once, with that position as P, before the end of
  B's own committed records; the message says to cut the stream at P.
  `StaleFirstBlockProbe OUT sameid exact` (2026-11-28): B's last
  committed record ends at 400,000; "The stream's records end at stream
  position 397512"; the stream cut at P as the message says, and the
  start after it: "THE CUT AT P LOST COMMITTED RECORDS", two synced puts.
  With another database's block (`other`), which is passed over, P is at
  the end of the database's own records.
* **A's records are taken.** If A's bytes begin where a record of both
  begins (the backup's end is one; later ones if B's records have the
  sizes of A's) and their times follow B's, the start takes A's records:
  the database holds transactions that the restore undid.
  `StaleCopyProbe OUT restore` (B has committed nothing since the copy):
  "B started: of its 8 keys, 8 read a value that A wrote after the copy";
  `same` (two copies written side by side with one clock): the same,
  after B's own 13 records.
* **Refused with no way out.** If A's records are earlier than B's
  before them, as after a restore: `StaleCopyProbe OUT earlier`,
  "non-increasing tt, tt:350000000, tpos:36416, ...", a
  `StreamFormatException` without P, the files as before.

It is beside 028-67's "Found, not covered" (a record whose later nodes
are another file's): there the header is the database's own, here the
whole record or block is a copy's. What would close it: a value in each
block's and record's check that a copy does not share once it is
written to again, such as an ID of the start that wrote it, or the check
of the record before it (a chain).

F2. Other bytes at a meta block's position, once taken, refuse a later start
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Severity S2, where another file's bytes can lie at a stream file's first
meta block. Shown by probes (`StaleFirstBlockProbe`, six variants;
`StartChain` with other bytes).

Its reach: 028-67's rule is for other bytes from any page boundary of
the last file (`DbTesterTail` puts them from every page; 035-02's test,
section 10.3, over the meta positions), and a new file's start is one.
035-02's kills left other bytes only from 2 MiB into a cut first write of
a new file, and zeros when the write was cut before that (its sections
7.1 and 9): on the devices measured there the first block of a file was
not reached, and a block further into a file is the first that a start
reads only if no block before it in the file is valid. So this is a gap
in the rule as the code and 027-03 state it, open on a storage that can
leave a deleted file's bytes at the start of a new file; not a case seen
on APFS.

Where: `RecoverableStream.readFirstMetaBlock`
(`java/src/bergdb/storage/RecoverableStream.java:1094`): it reads each
block from the first position on with `readMetaBlock` (:1113), which
throws for a block that does not begin with the magic bytes and this
version (:1378), and it takes the first block whose last byte is 10 as
the stream's first, null if that block does not hold its own position
(:1115). 028-76's fix passes over a block that is not valid by its last
byte, recovery's fill and a block of zeros; a block of other bytes is
neither. `leftoverProblem` (:498) refuses the same block as the first of
a file before a gap. 027-03, section 6, gives the argument that the
code's comment has: "The first block is the stream's own: a stream's
bytes are cut only from its end, and a kill's other bytes are in the
last file, from a page boundary on." It holds at the start after the
kill; the last file of that start is the first file of a later one.

How other bytes get there, with a start that takes the stream:

* **(a) Inside a record that the start aborts.** A kill cuts the first
  write of a new stream file, and the file holds other bytes from its
  start. The record that reaches over the file's first block has its
  header in the file before, so the header holds its check, and the walk
  takes the record as cut and completes it (`tornRecordIsOwn`, :397):
  its content is not read, and the other bytes at the block's position
  stay in the stream, inside an aborted record. That start and every
  later one pass over the block (`readOwnMetaBlock`), as 028-67 says.
* **(b) By the way out of a `BadTailException`.** If the last record
  before the other bytes ends exactly at the block's position, the walk
  refuses the tail with P 128 bytes after it (`verified = next`, :347 to
  :366: `getSpos` steps over the block). The message says to cut the
  stream at P: the file then keeps its first 128 bytes, the other bytes
  at the block's position, and the start takes it.

Then: reclaim's cut makes that file the stream's first (each file is the
first for a while if the cut comes to lie in it), and the next start
refuses the database, which ran and stopped cleanly since:

* other bytes that are not a meta block's: "Invalid magic bytes in
  stream: the stream is not a BergDB stream, or it is damaged ..."
  (`BadMagicBytes` from :1113);
* an older stream file's block of this database (valid, another
  position; the same offsets, as in 035-02, section 7.1): "Could not find
  *any* valid meta block. ... The stream begins at 800000, and the bytes
  before it are not there: it may lack bytes that the database needs";
* another database's block of the same position (valid, another ID): the
  stream's ID is taken from it, its own blocks are passed over, and the
  start refuses with "The meta block at 800000 names the last transaction
  at 797296, before the stream's first position, 800000: the stream lacks
  bytes that the database needs";
* a block of another version (2026.6's 155): "Cannot read meta block
  with version 155. ... Version 155 is a database written by BergDB
  release 2026.6, which this release does not open"
  (`checkBlockHeader`).

Nothing is written by the refused start, and no record is lost: every
byte of the database is there. None of the messages names a way out; two
of them say that bytes are missing, which they are not. After a power
loss in a cut's batch the same file as a file before a gap is refused by
028-66's rule ("holds the position 4200000, not its own: written at
another place, or damaged"), with that rule's way out.

The probe: `StaleFirstBlockProbe OUT STALE [exact]` is
`FileStreamPowerLossTester.testStartOnACutStreamThatBeginsAtAnInvalidBlock`
(028-76's test) with the file at the kill's block holding 300 other bytes
where the test has no file. Runs of 2026-11-28 on `ee8a8165`:

* `none` (the control, 028-76's case): start 1 ok, start 2 ok, every key
  as written.
* `random`, `older`, `other`, `v155` (the record over the block, case
  (a)): start 1 ok; after the cut, "START 2 REFUSED" with the four
  messages above, the files as before the start.
* `random exact`, `older exact` (case (b)): start 1 refused with a
  `BadTailException`, P = 400128; the stream cut at P as the message
  says, the file at 400000 128 bytes; start 1 ok; after the cut, "START 2
  REFUSED" as above.

What a fix has to settle: which blocks may lie at a meta position of a
stream that a start takes. Either the walk refuses other bytes at a meta
position inside the tail it takes (a cut record's blocks, and the block
after the last whole record, so that P is the block's position, not 128
after it), or every reader of a first block passes over what is not this
database's, with the ID from a source that other bytes cannot give. The
first is the smaller change and keeps 028-76's rule as it is; it makes a
start refuse, with P before the record, a tail that it takes today.

F3. A creation whose first write a kill left as zeros is not refused as one
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Severity S4. Shown by a probe (`CreationZerosProbe`).

Where: `FileStream.zeroTailLength`
(`java/src/bergdb/stream/FileStream.java:1868`) leaves a stream whose
only file is all zeros as it is (028-60: a repair never empties a
stream), and `RecoverableStream.isIncompleteCreation` (:591) takes only
bytes that begin as a meta block (:599). A kill of a creation's first
write that leaves zeros within the file's length (028-60's zeros begin at
a page boundary, and the creation's first write begins at the file's
start) gives a file of zeros only. The start refuses it with "Could not
find *any* valid meta block. At least the very first meta block must be
valid in a db stream.", a `StreamFormatException`, where every other cut
of a creation, with or without zeros after it, gives
`DatabaseNotCreatedException` and "Remove its files and start it again".
An application that removes the files of a database that was not created
does not see this one as such.

The probe's run of 2026-11-28: the creation (416 bytes, two records) cut
at 12 positions from 1 to 415 bytes, alone and followed by zeros to
4,096: `DatabaseNotCreatedException` in all 24; 0 bytes and zeros to
4,096: the `StreamFormatException`. No file changed.

F4. The bound of the zero tail holds for the default buffer only
----------------------------------------------------------------

Severity S4, the Core API only. By reading; not run.

Where: `FileStream.ZERO_TAIL_MAX`
(`java/src/bergdb/stream/FileStream.java:279`), 8 MB, with the reason in
its Javadoc: "A write of BufferedStream's thread is at most 5 MB". A
write is at most half of the buffer's largest size
(`java/src/bergdb/storage/BufferedStream.java:190` and :489), and 5 MB
is half of the default,
10 MB (`StorageManager.DEFAULT_BUFFER_SIZE`).
`DbBuilder.stream(name, stream, bufferSize, bufferTime)`
(`java/src/bergdb/core/DbBuilder.java:341`) takes any size: from 16 MB a
write can pass 8 MB, and a kill's zeros (028-60) of more than 8 MB are
left in the stream, where a start refuses them if they reach past a
record's end ("Bad transaction size in db stream, 0"). The Pub API has
no such setting. Either the builder bounds the size, or the open's bound
follows the buffer's.

F5. Notes on 028-76's decided case (a), from the probes
--------------------------------------------------------

Not a new finding: 028-76's refusal of a file before a gap whose first
block is recovery's fill, decided (a) for 2026.7 (Frans, 2026-11-28).
What the probes add:

* The same refusal for a first block that a kill cut inside (1 to 127
  bytes written) and recovery completed: the block then holds the
  database's ID and position and a last byte of 0, and the message says
  "is not valid, its last byte 0: damaged". `StartChain` counts both as
  the decided case.
* How often a kill ends the stream at a file's first block: the stream
  moves to a new file between an append that fills the last file and the
  first append to the new one, with the forces of the full file, the new
  file and the directory between them (`FileStream.newFile`), so a kill
  of a busy writer lands there for the time of those forces in each
  file's writing, not only at one instant. `week.sh`'s Kill app met it at
  its 30th kill.


2. Checked and found right
==========================

By reading and by `StartChain` (section 3), in the environment of cut
tails and zeros, with kills and power losses in a cut's batch:

* **Nothing before the decision.** `recover()` reads the last valid
  block, checks the record it names, the files before a gap and the tail
  (`walkTail`) before `fixMetaBlockIfNeeded` and the completion; a start
  refused by these left every file as it was in every run. A start that
  `Reclaim.initFromDb` refuses for a first position after the cut has
  written recovery's completion before, as `FileStream`'s Javadoc says.
* **The completion is taken again.** After a kill at any byte of what a
  start's recovery appended (the cut block's completion, the size
  field's, the zeros, the meta blocks in them, new files), with and
  without zeros after it, and after a stop with no commit, where
  `FileStream`'s open leaves the completion's zeros out: the next start
  takes the stream and holds what the stream held.
* **The first valid block.** A cut stream holds a valid block of its own
  after its first position: the cut is at or before the record that the
  last block written names (`Reclaim.cutRow`, `metaTransPos >= row.pCut`),
  that block is before the cut's record, and the cut is made when the
  stream is durable past the record. A start's cut (`initFromDb`) is the
  same cut, read from a record that is in the stream. The blocks that a
  completion writes inside a record name the record that the last valid
  block named, at or after the cut.
* **The zero tail.** A committed record and a valid block end in 10, so
  the open leaves out only a completion's zeros, an aborted record's and a
  fill's, which the next recovery writes again as they were. A write of
  `BufferedStream` is at most 5 MB with the default buffer, below the
  8 MB bound (F4 for another buffer). The last file's
  size is taken again from its length without the zeros, and the file is
  cut before it takes an append or stops being the last.
* **The gap.** Any subset of the deletes of a cut's batch and of the
  start's own batch after it, with the tail cut by a kill at or after
  the cut's record: the start takes the files after the last gap, checks
  and deletes the files before it, and leaves no gap; refused only in
  028-76's decided case (a).
* **A tail of other bytes** (random bytes, an older stream file of the
  chain at the same offsets, the file's own bytes of 4,096 or 8,192
  before) after a kill at any position: taken as a stream cut at the
  kill, or refused with a `BadTailException` and no file changed; cut at
  its P, the stream started and held what it held at the kill, in every
  case. No such tail was refused with another exception. One shape is
  taken with its last record: a kill in a record's padding or at its last
  byte, with other bytes that end in 10. The record's header and nodes
  are the database's own then; only its last byte, which no check covers,
  is not.
* **The creation.** A cut in the first meta block, in either record or
  between them (12 positions), with or without zeros after it, refuses
  with `DatabaseNotCreatedException` and writes nothing, as 028-18
  decided; but F3.

Known limits met and left as they are: a hole in the last append (zeros
with later bytes intact; 014-01, 005-83), which the walk does not find
in a record before the last, as it parses only the last committed record
whole; zeros past a record's end in a run of more than 8 MB (refused,
`Database`'s Javadoc).


3. The probes and their runs
============================

In `2026-11-28-start-probes/`, each in the package `bergdb.core`, with
its usage in its header; compiled against `java/build/bergdb.jar` of
`ee8a8165` and run with `-ea` on F53 beside `week.sh`. Kind: functional.
`runs-2026-11-28.txt` beside them has the commands and their output.

* `StartChain OUT FIRST LAST STEPS [RETAIN [STOP [SCALE [STALE]]]]`:
  chains of faults on one file database, each start checked against a
  model of the keys: kills at chosen positions (the last record's header,
  a meta block, a file's start, right after a cut's record), with zeros
  or other bytes after them, kills inside a recovery's own writes, power
  losses in a cut's batch (any subset of the files taken off). Its forces
  are no-ops; the faults are made on the files. After each commit it
  checks that the files begin at or before reclaim's cut.
* `StaleFirstBlockProbe`, `StaleCopyProbe`, `CreationZerosProbe`: F2, F1
  and F3.

`StartChain`'s runs of 2026-11-28, each start of a chain on what the
fault before it left:

* **No other bytes, files of 200 kB** (400 chains of 20 steps): 8,000
  starts after 5,446 kills, 1,141 kills in a recovery's writes and 2,624
  power losses in a batch; 87 refusals of 028-76's decided case (a); no
  other refusal, no wrong value, no file changed by a refused start.
* **No other bytes, files of 200 kB, 1 MB and 5 MB** (100 chains of 70
  steps, a retain duration of 250 s, values five times larger): 7,000
  starts after 4,954 kills, 860 kills in a recovery's writes and 786
  power losses; 33 of the decided case; nothing else.
* **No other bytes, per-append forcing off, files of 200 kB** (300
  chains of 20 steps): 6,000 starts after 4,096 kills, 839 kills in a
  recovery's writes and 1,975 losses of a part of a batch's deletes; 92
  of the decided case; nothing else.
* **Other bytes after a quarter of the kills, files of 200 kB** (400
  chains of 20 steps): 7,987 starts; of 768 tails of other bytes, 408
  were refused with a `BadTailException`, cut at its P and then started,
  and 325 were taken as they were; none was refused otherwise, and no
  start held another value than the stream held at the kill. 9 chains
  later met F2: 7 by a file before a gap, 2 by the stream's first block
  ("Could not find *any* valid meta block"); 123 of the decided case.

Runs of the probe's earlier builds, with the same faults: 20,020 starts
without other bytes and 22,318 with. Their outcomes are those above and
two that the last build takes into account: two cuts made in one record
(the lowest position of a kill is then that record's end), and a kill
in a record's padding or last byte with other bytes that end in 10
(section 2).
